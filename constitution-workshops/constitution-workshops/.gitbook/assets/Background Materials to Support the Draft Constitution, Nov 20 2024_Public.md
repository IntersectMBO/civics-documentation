# Prerequisite Reading {#prerequisite-reading}

To fully understand the constitution, please read about the newly-implemented on-chain governance system specified in the [Cardano Improvement Proposal 1694](https://github.com/cardano-foundation/CIPs/tree/master/CIP-1694).

# The community review process to-date {#the-community-review-process-to-date}

On behalf of the Cardano Community, the Cardano Civics Committee of Intersect brought together representatives of the Cardano Community, blockchain governance and constitutional scholars and the Cardano Parameter Committee to develop a draft Cardano Constitution to give effect to the community-based governance model as contemplated by CIP 1694 and approved by the Cardano Community.  The draft Constitution that was the work-product of this collective effort was then submitted in July, 2024 to representatives from the Cardano Community who volunteered to participate in a series of global community workshops. Between July and October, 63 workshops were held in locations all over the world and involved more than 1,400 participants. 

Each workshop, as well as others from the Cardano Community via an online open form, reviewed and provided feedback and recommendations on the July draft.  Each workshop also elected a Constitutional Delegate and Alternate to represent the views of their workshop throughout the drafting process and at the Constitutional Convention in the first week of December, 2024\.

As part of helping to organize the 63 global workshops, the Civics Committee identified 15 of the most complex issues that arose from the initial drafting process that the Civics Committee asked workshops to provide feedback on.  Each workshop chose which issues they particularly wanted to focus on. Output from the workshops, together with open-ended community feedback, shaped the edits that have been made since.

The workshop data was reviewed by the Cardano Civics Committee, the Parameters Committee and the group of blockchain governance and constitutional scholars and turned into proposed edits.  The Delegates and Alternates met in a series of online and in person workshops to synthesize the output from the global workshops and review the resulting proposed revisions to the draft Constitution.  In these workshops, Delegates were asked to review the proposed changes in light of their workshop’s feedback and work as a group to settle on the best path forward. This often took the form of debates, solutioning, and compromise. Using that output, the Delegates, again working with the Civics Committee, the Parameter Committee and a group of blockchain governance and constitutional scholars, revised the July draft Constitution.  That work has resulted in the November \[20\] draft Constitution which is being released to the Cardano Community.  As noted above, the Delegates will continue to review and potentially propose additional revisions to the November \[20\] draft.  A final draft Constitution will be submitted to the Delegates for approval in Buenos Aires and Nairobi at the Constitutional Convention.  The Constitutional Convention will be live-streamed to the entire Cardano Community.

In the Delegate workshops, the Delegates also identified a number of future governance workstreams. The Cardano Community will be invited to organize and participate during 2025 in these various future governance workshops.  These future workshops will focus on various governance considerations such as governance processes and procedures that should not appropriately be included in a constitution, governance processes that the Constitution requires the community to implement to give effect to various constitutional mandates, and potential future amendments to the Constitution that may become appropriate based on review and feedback as to how governance processes are working.

# The role of the Cardano Constitution {#the-role-of-the-cardano-constitution}

Constitutions provide the highest level of governance framing \- the rules for making rules.  Most  nation states have their own constitutions. In the blockchain industry, very few blockchain protocol constitutions exist and those that do, tend to narrowly focus solely on governance activities that can be self-implemented on-chain through smart contract or similar approaches.

The Cardano Constitution aspires to go beyond what other blockchain protocols have attempted to date, recognizing that that the Cardano Constitution is not a constitution for only a blockchain but rather, it is a constitution for a blockchain ecosystem – a much more ambitious endeavor.  Accordingly, how on-chain governance actions are approved, while extremely important, is not the sole focus of the Constitution. Rather, the Constitution provides the basis and fundamental framework through which all participants in the Cardano Community can come together to  govern themselves and form radically new approaches to human interaction and collaboration.

While Cardano is not a traditional nation state and does not strive to become one, this Constitution recognizes that the Cardano Community, in voluntarily coming together, represents a virtual nation that seeks to create its own form of self governance.  
Accordingly, the role of the Cardano constitution is twofold. Firstly, it establishes rights, expectations and principles of governance behavior for the Cardano Blockchain ecosystem. This is enforced through voluntary actions of the community self-reinforcing constitutional behavior. Second, the Constitution  provides technical safeguards, such as minimum and maximum parameter settings. The safeguards are enforced by a smart contract or the Cardano ledger where possible. Where it is not possible, the Constitutional Committee (or CC) can prevent unconstitutional governance actions from being enacted on-chain.

The Cardano Constitution is designed to protect the Cardano Blockchain by requiring that governance actions take into account the impact of such governance actions on the security, performance, functionality and long-term sustainability of the Cardano Blockchain; by  laying out principles of good governance within Cardano’s CIP-1694 governance structure; and by inspiring and encouraging  the community to uphold the shared principles that make the Cardano Community unique.

# The Contents and Structure of the Cardano Constitution

The Constitution consists of three parts:  the Preamble, the Articles of the Constitution and the Guardrails Appendix. The Constitution also recognizes the governance bodies of the Cardano Blockchain ecosystem:  DReps, SPOs and the Constitutional Committee.  Each of the parts of the Constitution and the governing bodies recognized in the Constitution are discussed below.

# Introductory Note: Why Does the Constitution include both Articles and a Guardrails Appendix?

**The Structure of the Cardano Constitution**  
As noted, the Cardano Constitution includes both Articles and a Guardrails Appendix.    
The preamble and articles provide the governing framework for Cardano. The Articles establish the governance rights and processes for the Cardano Community.  The Articles, as discussed below, include tenants which establish foundational guidance as to how the Cardano Blockchain is intended to operate and be maintained.  fulfill a function akin to national constitutions by establishing how people govern themselves. The Articles recognizes the branches of Cardano’s government–DReps, SPOs and the CC–and sets out their respective rights and responsibilities.  The Articles establish the rights of the owners of ada to participate in on-chain governance.  The Articles also establish the role that Cardano budgets will play in the future operations of the Cardano Blockchain and recognize the rights of the Cardano Community to access the Cardano Treasury in the future. Finally, the Articles provide the mechanics by which owners of ada can amend the Constitution in the future.

The guardrails are set forth in the Guardrails Appendix to the Constitution. They fulfill the unique function of governing a technology that traditional constitutions lack. Governance actions can change the settings of the Cardano Blockchain. The guardrails put limits on these changes to keep the Cardano Blockchain within operational boundaries.  These operational details are included in the Guardrails as an Appendix in order to keep the Constitution as a whole more approachable. An appendix denotes additional details that the less interested reader can skip.

**Why the Guardrails are part of the Constitution**   
The primary reason Guardrails are included in the Constitution is for enforceability. Before on-chain governance actions may be implemented on chain, the CC must review the constitutionality of the proposed governance actions. The CC therefore ensures that all governance actions are in accordance with both the Article and Guardrails Appendix. The CC’s remit does not extend to any other documents (described in [CIP-1694](https://github.com/cardano-foundation/CIPs/tree/master/CIP-1694)). If the Guardrails Appendix were moved to a separate document outside of the Constitution, the Guardrails would only be voluntarily enforced by the community, adding significant operational risk into the governance system of Cardano.

The Cardano Community absolutely retains the right to change Guardrails by amending the Constitution.  Because the Guardrails Appendix is deemed to be a part of the Constitution–thus requiring that a change to the Guardrails be implemented through an amendment to the Constitution–assures that the Guardrails always remain aligned with the rest of the Constitution. Additionally, including the Guardrails within the Constitution recognizes the relationship between the Gurdrails Appendix and the rest of the Constitution. Including the Guardrails within the Constitution assures consistency between the Constitution and Guardrails script. CIP-1694 allows Constitutional amendments to update both the Guardrails script and Constitution text in the same governance action. This encourages both to be updated in tandem to keep the text and code aligned. Removing the Guardrails Appendix from the Constitution text would leave no logical or enforceable connection between the Guardrails script and the Constitution. 

Two additional considerations are worth noting. First, the Constitution amendment provisions of Article VII allow for different voting thresholds for amending Articles of the Constitution and amending specific Guardrails contained in the Guardrails Appendix.  Second, many Guardails include parameters that can be changed from time to time. This changes can be made by the ada holders through on-chain governance actions without triggering an amendment to the Constitution.

# A Section by Section Summary of the Constitution as Well as the Edits Resulting from Workshop Feedback and Discussions with Constitutional Delegates

# The Preamble {#the-preamble}

**What is the Purpose of a Preamble?**  
The preamble of a constitution helps to set the aspirations and very high level principles of the governing system and constituents. Often preambles are intentionally inspirational and set intent for the constitution itself.

**How Should a Preamble be Interpreted**  
In practice, some courts use preambles to guide interpretation but others do not. In most cases, the articles of a constitution provide greater detail and are used as the primary source for decision making.  However, there may be cases where articles do not provide sufficient clarity. Unless the constitution dictates that the preamble should not be used to provide interpretational  guidance, courts are free to use the preamble as an additional source of guidance in interpreting the articles of the constitution.  

**Minimal Principles**  
Because the principles within a preamble are typically very high level, they can be interpreted subjectively. As a result, preambles often seek to keep a minimal number of principles to avoid contradictions. 

**The Cardano Constitution Preamble**  
The Preamble for the Cardano Constitution establishes high-level principles around which the Cardano Community aligns. It is also intended to be inspirational in the vision it contains and its encouragement for the Cardano Community to come together and participate in Cardano’s governance.  

In response to broad community feedback that the Preamble needed to be more inspirational, the Cardano Civics Committee, in direct consultation with many community members and Constitutional Delegates, redrafted the Preamble as reflected in the current draft to include:

* “Why” Cardano  
* Principles  
* A definition of the ecosystem, community and its participants  
* The purpose of a Constitution for Cardano

When polled, the Constitutional Delegates’ top principles were transparency, decentralization, security, trust and accountability. Delegates overall wanted to keep some history in the Preamble, which has been included in the Framing Notes

It should be noted that there is nothing in the Preamble that precludes the Constitutional Committee from looking to the Preamble as it interprets the Constitution. It should also be noted that the Framing Notes which, beside providing historical background, provide some thoughts on how to approach the Constitution.  The Framing Notes, however, are not intended to be a part of the Constitution and the Constitutional Committee may not look to the Framing Notes as it interprets the Constitution.

# Article I:  Principles versus Tenets {#article-i:-principles-versus-tenets}

The preamble sets out principles, who the constitution applies to, and frames basic governing structures. Following the preamble is a set of tenets in Article I. Because principles can be subjectively interpreted, the tenets contain more details to support the Constitutional Committee’s interpretation of governance actions. The tenets provide technical direction to ensure that future Cardano Blockchain development does not violate certain rights or principles.  

Tenets are intended to provide more explicit guidance to the Constitutional Committee than principles. That is, governance actions are intended to be evaluated in accordance with the Tenets, but if the Tenets do not provide sufficient or appropriate guidance, the Constitutional Committee may also consider the principles contained in the preamble.

# Section by Section Edits Resulting from Workshop Feedback and Discussions with Constitutional Delegates {#section-by-section-edits-resulting-from-workshop-feedback-and-discussions-with-constitutional-delegates}

## Preamble {#preamble}

In response to broad community feedback that the preamble needed to be more inspirational, the Cardano Civics Committee, in direct consultation with many community members and Constitutional Delegates, redrafted the preamble as you see in the current draft. It includes:

* “Why” Cardano  
* Principles  
* A definition of the ecosystem, community and its participants  
* The purpose of a Constitution for Cardano

When polled, the Constitutional Delegates’ top principles were transparency, decentralization, security, trust and accountability. Delegates overall wanted to keep some history in the preamble, which has been included in the framing note.

## Article I. Cardano Blockchain Tenets and Guardrails {#article-i.-cardano-blockchain-tenets-and-guardrails}

Workshop participants and delegates felt aligned to the tenets at their core. Workshop participants wanted simpler language. Implementation comments that referenced specific technologies were removed, as preferred by Delegates. Workshop participants wanted to explicitly add a 45BN ada supply cap in the constitution, as did Delegates. It was added as a new tenet.

## Article II. The Cardano Blockchain Community {#article-ii.-the-cardano-blockchain-community}

Article II was not the focus of any workshop questions, but it became clear that the actors were not clear enough. The first version of the draft referenced the Cardano Community, members, the Cardano Blockchain Ecosystem, ada owners, ada holders, users etc. In the latest version, this has been simplified to the Cardano Blockchain Ecosystem, composed of people and technology, where the participants are collectively the Cardano Community. Ada holders have been removed, leaving only references to ada owners in the articles.

## Article III. Participatory And Decentralized Governance {#article-iii.-participatory-and-decentralized-governance}

**Authorized designees and voting rights**: Workshop participants were largely in favor of allowing ada owners to authorize designees to exercise their voting rights. This sets the expectation that ada owners who use third parties, such as custodians, maintain their governance rights and get to choose whether or not the third party can vote on their behalf.

Allowing authorized designees was a highly debated topic among Constitutional Delegates. The updated text is the best solution that could be found. Many delegates maintain the opinion that the only guarantee of voting rights is to self-custody ada. While this may be the case, the text still protects the voting rights of ada owners who use third party services to hold their ada.

Delegates specifically wanted the language to be clear that ada owners may *either* provide or withhold authorization from third parties to vote on their behalf. For example, an ada owner that uses a centralized exchange (CEX) could request that CEX not exercise voting rights with the ada in their account. In the future, third party services may build tooling to support ada owners to opt into voting, similar to how CEX users can opt into staking. Until then, user agreements can be used to grant or revoke voting rights.

Technical solutions for enabling third parties to vote on an ada owner’s behalf has been identified as a critical future workstream.

**Budget process**: Workshop participants expressed interest in having more details about the Cardano Budget process, specifically who would administer the budget. Delegates agreed that the details of the budget process belong outside of the constitution until the process matures. They agreed to add a requirement that Cardano budgets must identify one or more administrators. Delegates were concerned with naming an administrator in the Constitution because they wanted to ensure there is flexibility over time as to who submits a Cardano Budget.  

In addition, workshop participants indicated that they wanted to ensure contracts funded by the Cardano Treasury include dispute resolution provisions. This was included in the text as a requirement for all treasury withdrawals. 

**Budget Categories:** Workshop participants wanted to ensure the Cardano Budget covered Constitutional Committee administrative costs and contingency funding. A contingency fund was seen as an emergency measure to access funds quickly. Delegates agreed that contingency planning could be handled as part of a responsible budget process rather than a constitutional requirement. Article VI provides for CC administrative costs to be reimbursed if approved by ada owners.

A future workstream was identified to ensure the budget process follows best practices, including contingency planning.

## Article IV. Delegated Representatives {#article-iv.-delegated-representatives}

**DRep Codes of Conduct:** Workshop participants strongly wanted DRep codes of conduct. There was varying interest for codes of conduct to be the same for all DReps or on-chain. Since DReps are expected to take diverse forms, a one-size-fits-all approach didn’t make sense at this stage. The constitution now says that DReps are expected to adopt a Code of Conduct. The wording ‘expected’ recognizes that it is up to the ada owners to select DReps who publish an acceptable Code of Conduct.

**DRep compensation:** Workshop participants were comfortable that DRep compensation should be allowed, but there was no consensus for how to compensate DReps, or the appropriate funding source. Delegates settled on saying the “Dreps may be compensated” as it allows for both voluntary and paid DReps. Workshop data made it clear that large parts of the community do not have the luxury of participating as a DRep without compensation, while others felt that DReps should be driven by people fulfilling their civic duty. The language allows for both, and an important future workstream is for the community to define DRep compensation mechanisms and funding sources.

## Article V. Stake Pool Operators {#article-v.-stake-pool-operators}

**SPO Codes of Conduct:** Workshop participants largely agreed that it would be beneficial for SPOs to adopt a code of conduct given their new role in on-chain voting. However, some people were concerned that the new requirements may disrupt SPO operations. Because SPOs have been successfully operating without Codes of Conduct for years, SPOs are only ‘encouraged’ to adopt a Code of Conduct.

**Separation of powers:** Workshop participants did not demonstrate a majority opinion for separation of powers between SPOs and DReps. This was a key concern for many Delegates. Through discussions about technical enforcement, precedent setting, and relying on informed choices, text was added to require SPOs and DReps to disclose when voting on in both capacities before casting the vote. Improving the separation of powers is captured as a future workstream. The workstream is expected to minimally include monitoring the separation of powers across DReps, SPOs and the CC to inform ada owners’ delegation choices.

## Article VI. Constitutional Committee {#article-vi.-constitutional-committee}

**Constitutional Committee (CC) Size and Term Length:** Workshop participants wanted the constitution to clarify how the size and term length would be set for the CC, rather than specifying the numbers directly in the text. Delegates were happy that the CC term length and size be determined by ada holders through on-chain actions.

**CC Elections:** Workshop participants expressed interest in more details about the CC election within the constitution, but for the purpose of understanding the process better. Delegates agreed that CC election details should not yet be included in the constitution \- the community needs time to experiment with the off-chain election process.

In all cases, the CC election concludes with a governance action where DReps and SPOs elect a new CC, replacing the previous committee on-chain. Additional off-chain processes ahead of on-chain voting may be developed. The CC election is a necessary future workstream for 2025, and must be executed by mid-2025 before the Interim Constitutional Committee’s original term expires.

**The role of the Constitutional Committee (CC):** The text in sections 4 and 5 intends to more accurately reflect the technological implementation of CIP-1694. The text permits, but does not require, the CC to vote on info actions with the exception of an info action containing the Cardano Budget or pursuant treasury withdrawals. 

Because an info action does not trigger an on-chain implementation, the CC’s vote indicates their opinion, but does not stop the info action from existing on-chain. The wording helps to clarify that the CC’s vote may indicate their opinion as to the constitutionality of the contents if they were to be implemented in the future. The text also specifies that the CC shall vote on the Cardano Budget via an info action. Delegates were comfortable allowing CC votes on info actions, with the exception of the Cardano Budget and pursuant treasury withdrawals, to be optional. A future workstream was identified to consider if the Budget approval could be its own info action in the future.

**CC Code of Conduct:** Workshop participants wanted the Cardano Community to have oversight of the CC’s Code of Conduct. Delegates and agreed to require a CoC for CC members. Enforcement will be handled by ada owners choosing whether or not to vote in a CC member by assessing their posted Code of Conduct or in extreme cases through triggering a vote of no-confidence.

**Constitutional Committee Compensation:** Workshop participants strongly supported CC compensation, as did Delegates. Wording was included that specifically says that CC members *may* be compensated. This allows any CC member not wishing to be compensated to avoid it.

## Article VII. Amendments {#article-vii.-amendments}

Although discussed in several forums, the idea of creating different amendment thresholds for the guardrails was not adopted. This was a combination of not having a technical solution available and that the Parameter Committee did not make a formal recommendation to do so. However, the language is flexible enough to adopt different amendment thresholds in the future.

Separately, the Delegates surfaced the concern that the constitution should be positioned as a living document. This is because many details were intentionally omitted from the constitution. This allows time for the community to learn and mature processes before enshrining them in the constitution. The delegates wanted to make it clear that the constitution should be reviewed against our lessons learned as a community.  Language has been added that indicates the constitution is a living document. The general sentiment is that we can expect more amendments in the first years, yielding to a stable document over time as seen with many national constitutions.

## Appendix I. Cardano Blockchain Guardrails {#appendix-i.-cardano-blockchain-guardrails}

**Parameter guardrails review:** The Parameter Committee proposed several changes. All were related to the guardrail descriptions. None of the guardrail values were changed.

# FAQs {#faqs}

1. How can the constitution be changed over time?  
   1. The Constitution contains Article VII that explains amendments. The constitution is amended by an on-chain governance action with the approval of the Constitutional Committee (67%) and DReps (75%).  The amendment threshold for DReps can be changed within the limits of the guardrails (65% \- 90%).  
2. What is the final ratification process for the Cardano Constitution?  
   1. The draft constitution must be approved by a majority of the Constitutional Delegates at the Constitutional Convention. The approved version will be put on chain as a “New Constitution” governance action. This will require approval of 67% of the Interim Constitutional Committee and 75% of DReps.  
3. Why are details that cannot be enforced on-chain included in the constitution?  
   1. This constitution aims to both protect the blockchain network, set behavioral expectations and attract people to the ecosystem. Expanding beyond the technology differentiates Cardano by setting lasting ground-rules for how the community both interacts and governs itself.
