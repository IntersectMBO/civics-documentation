# CIP-149 grant programme

CIP-149 lets a wallet offer its users a simple way to donate to their DRep, one route to compensating the people who do the work of governance. The feature only matters if wallets actually build it. The Civics Committee ran a grant programme to get it built, and to gather the data needed to judge whether it works.

### The programme

The Civics committee approved a **100,000 ADA grant programme** on [18 September 2025](../meeting-minutes/2025-civics-meeting-minutes/civics-meeting-mintues-sep-18-2025.md), by unanimous vote, expanding it beyond wallet providers to include the governance tools that build delegation transactions and the SDKs that provide reference implementations.

Two design choices made it a genuine experiment rather than a giveaway:

* **Post-payment.** Grants were paid only after a team completed the work and proved it functioned, not up front ([4 September 2025](../meeting-minutes/2025-civics-meeting-minutes/civics-meeting-minutes-sep-4-2025.md)).
* **Data reporting.** Participating teams were required to report anonymised usage data, such as how many users were shown the feature and how many used it, so the ecosystem could measure whether optional DRep donations actually change behaviour ([14 August 2025](../meeting-minutes/2025-civics-meeting-minutes/civics-meeting-minutes-aug-14-2025.md)).

See the [grant programme](https://docs.intersectmbo.org/intersect-knowledge-base/archive/funding-administration-archive/intersect-administration-services/2025-apply-for-tender/open-tenders/civics-committee/cip-149-optional-drep-compensation) for the full criteria.

### Uptake

By April 2026, the Eternl wallet had completed and claimed the grant. The low uptake is itself a finding: it tells the committee something about how much appetite wallet teams currently have for building optional governance features, and it feeds the committee's wider work on governance incentives.
