### Decisions/Actions

#### Decisions Made

* The committee polled on SPO participation in off-chain Constitutional Committee elections and favoured it, 5 in favour, 1 against, 1 abstention. Final implementation is deferred pending confirmation that Ekklesia can support it and a recommendation on the tallying mechanism.
* The committee agreed the year-end schedule: skip 10 December for the annual members meeting, hold a final meeting on 17 December, then a holiday break from 18 December with the next meeting on 7 January (24 and 31 December cancelled).

| 5. CC election voting method and SPO participation | Thomas Lindseth opened whether to keep DReps as the electorate for CC elections or add SPOs or move to direct ADA-holder voting. Nicolas Cerny and Megan Dyamond supported SPO participation, noting the Conway hard fork gave SPOs a governance role and that their votes are required for governance actions. Jose Miguel De Gamboa and Nana Safo opposed it, arguing an SPO's mandate is block production, that CIP-1694 intentionally excluded SPOs from CC voting, and that delegating a single decision to two bodies double-counts trust. SPO active voting stake is roughly 10 billion ADA against about 5 billion for DReps, so a stake-weighted tally would be needed to balance power. | A poll of members present returned 5 in favour, 1 against, 1 abstention for SPO participation in off-chain elections. Thomas noted the constitution mandates an election without specifying the mechanism, so off-chain methods are permissible. Ian Hartwell noted Ekklesia has since run weighted and ranked-choice tallies. CIP-179 should be avoided because SPOs cannot easily use cold credentials. CIP-181 will change DRep delegation and staking rewards. Thomas will ask Adam whether Ekklesia can support this. |
